Nepal’s culinary landscape is far more diverse than just a standard "mixture" of a few common styles. With over 125 distinct ethnic and caste groups spread across extreme geographical micro-climates—from sub-tropical Terai plains to alpine Himalayan peaks—many smaller indigenous communities have highly specialized, independent culinary traditions.

The **Thami people** (an indigenous ethnic group mainly inhabiting parts of the eastern and central mid-hills, such as Dolakha and Sindhupalchok) are a great example, alongside several other distinct groups with their own culinary identities:

---

### 21. Thami (Thalang) Cuisine

The Thami community possesses a traditional diet deeply rooted in forest foraging, hill farming, and indigenous rituals:

* **Kodo and Makkai Dhindo:** Coarse, thick porridges made from finger millet or maize flour, serving as the primary daily energy source.
* **Sisnu ko Jhol:** A nutrient-dense, vitamin-rich wild stinging nettle soup.
* **Kinema & Gundruk preparations:** Naturally fermented soybean pastes and leafy greens used to add deep, savory umami flavors to simple hill vegetable broths.
* **Local Forest Yam & Root Curries:** Seasonally foraged wild tubers harvested from the Mahabharat slopes, boiled or roasted over open wood fires.
* **Jaand / Chhyang:** Home-brewed fermented millet or maize beer, central to all Thami life-cycle celebrations, community labor exchanges, and ancestral worship.

---

### 22. Lohorung Cuisine (Eastern Nepal)

The Lohorung, an indigenous Kiranti group hailing primarily from the Sankhuwasabha district, have a distinct culinary system separate from larger neighboring groups:

* **Wachisi:** A bitter-flavored traditional broth made using charred plant parts and local herbs.
* **Bhutika:** Specially prepared local rice dishes cooked with indigenous aromatic roots.
* **Sibrung:** Traditional festive and ritual food items made from ground grains and local meats.
* **Tongba & Local Brews:** Deeply embedded in their hospitality, featuring naturally fermented millet beverages.

---

### 23. Danuwar Cuisine (River Valleys of Central/Eastern Nepal)

The Danuwar are an indigenous community traditionally settled along major river basins (like the Sun Koshi and Dudh Koshi):

* **River Fish Specialties:** Because of their historic proximity to rivers, their cuisine features a wide variety of fresh river fish cooked with wild garlic, turmeric, and local chilies.
* **Bhakka & Rice-Flour Cakes:** Steamed fluffy cakes made from freshly milled rice flour, eaten warm.
* **Taro and Pumkin Leaf Delicacies:** Pounded leaf parcels stuffed with spiced lentil pastes, then shallow-fried or steamed in mustard oil.

---

### 24. Rajbanshi / Meche Cuisine (Jhapa and Morang, Eastern Terai)

Inhabiting the flat alluvial plains of far-eastern Nepal, the Rajbanshi and Meche people have a distinct food culture tied closely to wetland and riverine ecosystems:

* **Kachki Machha ko Jhol:** Curries made from tiny, whole freshwater river minnows spiced with heavy amounts of garlic, cumin, and mustard paste.
* **Marsa & Local Puffed Grains:** Traditional preparations of native grains harvested from the eastern plains.
* **Siltung & Local Terai Chutneys:** Pungent seed and wild-herb relishes ground on stone slabs (*silauto*).

---

### Summary: Mixture vs. Distinct Identities

While modern commercial restaurants in Kathmandu and major tourist hubs tend to homogenize Nepali food into a generalized menu of *Dal Bhat*, *Momos*, and *Chowmein*, rural and indigenous communities fiercely preserve their ancestral cooking techniques. Groups like the Thami, Lohorung, Danuwar, and the 20+ other ethnicities previously mentioned maintain distinct flavor profiles driven entirely by what grows—or can be foraged, hunted, and fermented—in their specific ancestral territories.

##  Creating a standard for Nepal's ethnic foods

The goal is to make Nepali ethnic foods a global brand, protect their authenticity, and boost the national economy. This is a two-part plan to solve the problems of weak branding and high barriers for new restaurants.

If Nepalis don't do it, someone else will, and 'steal' Nepal's global culinary branding. See the Indian "Momo King" brand that runs thousands of global franchisees under it's brand. No Nepali involvement.

Opportunities for which ethnic food? Newari, Thakali, Tharu, Tamang(?), Who else? Maybe aboriginal peoples?

### The Two Core Solutions

#### 1. Supply-Side: Building the Foundation

The main tool is the Restaurant Opening Template for each ethnic cuisine (Thakali, Newar, Tharu, etc.). This template is a complete business guide to make opening a restaurant easy and reliable.

|Template Component|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Menus & Recipes|Detailed, step-by-step instructions to ensure consistent quality and cultural accuracy.|
|Sourcing & Logistics|In-depth lists of regional farmers and suppliers to create a reliable supply chain for authentic ingredients.|
|Design & Ambiance|Guidelines for architecture, décor, and music to create an authentic cultural dining experience.|
|Training|Optional courses on cooking techniques, management, and food safety for new owners.|

Templates will be made in multiple tiers (basic, mid-range, upscale) to fit different budgets and will focus on flexibility for the owner's personal touch.

#### 2. Demand-Side: Creating Buzz and Branding

This will use a high-impact, low-cost marketing strategy.

- The Competition: A nationwide social media contest (YouTube/TikTok) where people submit videos showcasing their ethnic cuisine's recipes and restaurant concepts. This will create a massive amount of content to generate excitement and drive global interest.
    
- The Brand: Establish a "Nepali Select" certification (like Thailand's "Thai Select") to assure customers of quality and authenticity, combatting the "dilution" of brands like Thakali khana. This will be backed by a simple, voluntary checklist for adherence to core elements.
    
- Targeted Marketing: Use Nepali migrants abroad as "culinary ambassadors." Promote the food globally by stressing that it is unique and different from Indian/Chinese cuisine.
    

### Benefits

- Empowering Entrepreneurs: The templates significantly lower the cost and effort of opening an authentic ethnic restaurant, making it easier for new entrepreneurs, including Nepali migrants abroad.
    
- Cultural Preservation: The project involves deep field research and community workshops to document and preserve authentic traditional recipes and cooking techniques.
    
- Economic Growth: Creating a demand for authentic ethnic ingredients boosts local farmers and suppliers across all regions of Nepal, leading to increased local tourism and potential food exports.

### Getting Started

- Initial Focus: Start with a pilot project focused on the Thakali community to demonstrate success and create a unified brand and supply chain. Maybeee. Maybe other ethnicities? Whoever is the most willing!
    
- Funding: Seek support from the Ministry of Tourism, Nepal Tourism Board, local governments, and private investors.
