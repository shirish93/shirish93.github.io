title: My understanding of what leadership is

How do you start with leadership when you don't have an organization to lead? How do you build a community when all you have is friend groups. I think out aloud.

NOTE: This is semi-edited ramblings on my mentorship aspirations.

During the call with my sister earlier today I realized I have leadership aspirations. I haven't actually exploered that interest yet, never connected the dots. Turns out, community building and leadership involves aligning other people's goals with yours, and getting things done. Even if they dodn't know if that's what they want, or aren't sure if it'll necessarily work out well.

Turns out that the relationship between power, and how you get to it, can lead to corruption. The reason is: to become powerful, you need to convince other people that you are the one worthy of wielding power. And often  -- not just corrupt socties -- that can become transactional. A quid pro quo situation. I make you the president, you sign off on the contracts. You get the idea.

When you have collect too many quid pro quos, you are powerful, because you are owed a lot of favors. On the flip side, you also owe a lot of favors. So you either undermine the patronage network that got you power, or you undermine the goals of the system you're leading to reward the network that got you there. The needs of your patronage network are going to differ from the needs of the institution that you lead.

But then, how do you create an institutionalized power structure internally? How can you avoid the corruption that must necessarily come up with patronage networks? How can you rise without the patronage systems?

One potential approach is aligning with everybody on a shared vision. Relatedly, it would be a good idea to work towards a higher cause, where the exact vision may not align with others, but roughly the understanding of the future is shared. You find the people who believe on the higher cause, and work for it. Another approach could be to be an honest player, and earn trust as the 'honest guy'. Prove yourself through your technical skills, and your clean moral and ethical character. Be the person who's accountable to their stakeholders. Probably the harder way, considering organizational politics that take people to the top, but not impossible!

But most people don't work or network on quid pro quo basis, they're seeking a higher cause, a purpose. Are people really that transactional, don't they care? Maybe they don't. Isn't a shared vision of the future really challenging to create for a society that's so diverse and divergent? How do you bring together such diverse groups of people without bringing them together physically, how do you go beyond tribal leadership groups? How do you unite a people for a common cause without compromising on your fundamental vision and principles? I wonder.

One approach could be to establish (or enforce?) a shared identity. Identify (or create!) enemies and pretend to work towards the furthering of that shared identity. Strong nationalistic or ethnic identities are easy to start with. Strong nationalism by its definition must envision a very narrow potential future.

But that can't be enough! You can't just have a vision and share it with others. You also gotta commit to the cause, and work for it, do something for the cause to be put in power. But to what end? Surely the goal of power cannot be for its own self? Why would anybody vote for you if your election message was "Vote for me because I want to be the most voted person!"? Surely the end-goal of power is to accomplish a particular goal...using tools and means that become available after power is achieved?

First thing then, show people a future that's different from what exists, that you're going to create when you become powerful.  And you tell them how you're going to achieve it -- though that's not important for the most part. Finally, you convince people that the _only_ hindrance to the future state from being achieved is your lack of power and resources, and that they need to give it to you.

But it's not that easy. There's going to be opposition to your shared vision of future, and there's going to be competitors promising to achieve a very similar future without whatver baggage you might bring. We need to get a buy-in from the major stakeholders, and rise organically. An organic rise assures you don't encounter very disruptive or unexpected opposition that's hard to predict or deal with. It's not essential, but a really important good-to-have.

Tthat's my theory of high-level leadership. How does it relate to community building? What are the things that bring people together into a shared community? The two things that I've identified are food -- beyond common interest, because everybody loves to eat and everybody has trouble eating--, and some sort of entertainment. Which means, movies, board games, dances, drinking, and so forth any shared entertaining activity, even if it involves running an otherwise boring club.

What's the goal of community building? To create a community of people interested, invested in something. To come together for food and entertainment and/or shared activity because it's so much fun than doing it alone! But is that by itself sufficient? I think not. Such events might bring people together a few times, but won't help create a long-lasting community. What do you need for that is fulfilling the needs of their age group, which is a very vague statement statement. Let me expand on that.

If it's young people -- young single people -- they need a place to meet similar-thinking people of the preferred gender so they can get married or partnered. If it's partnered people, professional people, they need a place for professional goals. If it's people who are married, they need resources for childcare, maybe even a place for childcare and joint family outings with others. Perhaps opportunities for playdates, library activities, and so forth. Once they're older, they need a place to make friends for their kids and themselves. And then it's time to cater to their kids, the younger people who will go to summer camps and winter camps, and adventure trips etc. Teenagers and late-teens need something similar, but more exciting activities, and more opportunities to meet the preffered gender for romantic opportunities, once again.

Is that how it works? Is that how Scouts, and Churches, and weird political organizations, but also political parties, and WASPy communities work? I gotta think more about this.
